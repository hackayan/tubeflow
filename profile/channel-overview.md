# Channel overview: Ayan Das (@ayandas9197)

https://www.youtube.com/@ayandas9197

## What the channel is

A PhD researcher explaining computer science and neuroscience to students, and showing what research
work is actually like so more students try it.

## Content pillars

1. **Computer science explained.** Concepts students meet in coursework and in research: algorithms,
   machine learning, how models are built and tested.
2. **Neuroscience explained.** The brain for people who are not biologists yet, especially where it
   meets computing (computational neuroscience, brain-inspired AI).
3. **My PhD work.** What I am working on, explained simply: the question, why it matters, what
   failed, what I learned. TODO: one paragraph on your research topic so videos describe it accurately.
4. **Research motivation and skills.** How to start research as a student, read a paper, find a
   supervisor, handle rejection, keep going.

## Who it is for

Students, mostly undergraduates and master's students, in India and Bangladesh and anywhere else
English, Hindi or Bengali is spoken, who are curious about research but have not started yet.
Some are already in a PhD and want to know they are not the only one struggling.

## Languages

English by default. Hindi and Bengali versions where the audience asks for them: the comment and
geography data from `/learn` decides which videos get a translated version first.

## Formats

- **Long videos (8-20 min):** one concept or one research story per video.
- **Shorts (under 60 s):** cut from the long videos, or one idea and one surprising fact.

## Narration

AI text-to-speech for now, replaced by my own voice later.

## What the channel never does

- Claims about a paper or a result that the video cannot cite.
- Exaggerated health or "brain hack" claims from neuroscience.
- Sharing unpublished research data that the lab has not cleared.
