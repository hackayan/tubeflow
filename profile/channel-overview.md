# Channel overview: Ayan Das (@ayandas9197)

https://www.youtube.com/@ayandas9197

## What the channel is

An education channel. A PhD researcher teaches science, technology and engineering so students learn
it correctly, then go on to learn new things by themselves. Every video should leave the viewer
able to do or explain something they could not before, and wanting to learn the next thing.

## Content pillars

1. **Science and technology:** computer science, neuroscience, and the ideas underneath modern tech.
2. **Engineering:** how things are designed, built and tested, from first principles.
3. **Data analysis:** working with real data in Python: cleaning, statistics, visualisation, machine
   learning, and how not to fool yourself with numbers.
4. **Algorithmic trading:** markets, strategy logic, backtesting and risk, taught as data science
   and engineering. Education only, never financial advice (see writing-style.md).
5. **Interdisciplinary research:** where fields meet (brain + computing, data + finance, and so on),
   anchored in my own PhD work in computational neuroscience of psychosis: using mathematical and
   computational models of how the brain learns, predicts and updates beliefs (for example predictive
   coding and Bayesian or reinforcement-learning models) together with brain and behavioural data,
   to understand the mechanisms behind psychosis. TODO: add the specific question, methods and data
   of your thesis, and what is published or cleared to share.
6. **Learning and research skills:** how to learn a new field, read a paper, start research, and keep
   going when it is hard. The motivation comes from showing that it can be done, step by step.

Other science and engineering topics are welcome when the audience asks for them (`audience-learnings.md`)
and they can be taught correctly.

## Who it is for

Students, from school leavers to PhD students, in India and Bangladesh and anywhere else English,
Hindi or Bengali is spoken. They are curious but often learned things from rushed or wrong sources,
and many think research and advanced topics are "not for them".

## Teaching promise

- **Correct:** checked facts, cited sources, code that has actually been run.
- **Foundational:** start from what the viewer knows, build one step at a time, name the prerequisites.
- **Active:** every long video gives the viewer something to try (a question, an exercise, a dataset).
- **Honest:** says what is uncertain, what is simplified, and what usually goes wrong.

## Languages

English by default. Hindi and Bengali versions where the audience asks for them: the comment and
geography data from `/learn` decides which videos get a translated version first.

## Formats

- **Long videos (8-25 min):** one concept, one method or one worked project per video. Series for
  bigger subjects (for example data analysis from zero, or building a backtester step by step).
- **Shorts (under 60 s):** one idea, one misconception corrected, or one surprising fact, usually
  cut from a long video.

## Narration

AI text-to-speech for now, replaced by my own voice later.

## What the channel never does

- Claims about a paper, result or fact that the video cannot cite.
- Profit promises, "this strategy makes X% a month", buy or sell calls, or paid tips.
- Exaggerated health or "brain hack" claims.
- Code on screen that was not run, or results that were not reproduced.
- Sharing unpublished research data that the lab has not cleared.
